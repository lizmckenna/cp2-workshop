Civic Power Report 2 · in workshop · draft

Democracy needs an R&D function.

Democracy has a very limited R&D function. Most of the work runs on hunches, vendor pitches, self-reported survey data, and mobilizing counts that obscure much of the actual power-building work. Civic Power II can be an investment in changing that, i.e. in building the shared questions, evidence, and learning infrastructure the field has never had.

§ 1 · the frameWhat CP1 surfaced, and what CP2 has to do differently.

Better field is downstream of organizing capacity. Relational, deeper voter contact is not a tactic a firm can manufacture. It is a lagging indicator of organized base. CP1 gestured at this. CP2 has to land it.

Mass participation needs a converter. Without organized, formation-centered organizations channeling it, participation dissipates. The MN labor-faith-community alignment was exactly that. It turned broad participation into concentrated, directed power capable of moving legislation and governing.

Scale and depth are not opposites. But sequencing matters. You cannot rapid-respond your way to governing power. You can only rapid-respond from organized bases that already exist. CP2 publishes 18 months further into authoritarian consolidation, with more mobilization-without-translation to reckon with.

Capture is the live risk. Clean numbers get stripped from the conditions that produce them. They become memes, then funder targets, then the thing you have to walk back for years. CP2 has to publish in a way that makes capture harder, not easier.

Three things are doing the work. Conditions. Organization. Leadership. The variation we see across orgs (and inside orgs over time) lives in how those three interact.

§ 2 · the methodHow the question changes when the unit of analysis changes.

CP2 centers the organization as the unit of analysis. Individual data (attendance, network position, event sequences) becomes evidence about organizational properties. Same data, sharper questions. Each org is a deeply observed case.

Meso
Mediating institutions

Bigger than a single case, smaller than "how does democracy work?" The converters between participation and power.

Probabilistic
Not deterministic

Mechanisms shift the probability of conversion. They do not determine outcomes. Contingency continues to operate.

Evidence-paired
"How would we know?"

Every research question paired with the answer to: what evidence would tell us? No question without a data path.

What we're trying to deeply understand and explain: leadership.

Not as a wild card sitting next to the mechanisms. As the dependent variable. The production of purple line leaders (people with followings, embedded in relational fabric, who develop others) is what we are trying to deeply understand and explain.

§ 3 · the questionsNine questions, in working draft.

Three criteria for scoring. Field learning: how much does answering this teach the field? Practice: how much does it inform what organizers actually do? Paradigm shift: how much does it move elite, funder, and academic understanding? We won't run all nine in one study. The point of this room is to figure out which to keep.

§ 4 · the promptWhat would change your work?

For the room · anonymously or signed
If you had the answer one year from now, what question would change how (or how effectively) you do your work?
Not "what should the field study." What would change your next move. Specificity is more useful than scope.
